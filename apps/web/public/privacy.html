In short

You do not need an account, and we do not ask for your name, email, phone number or any other detail about you. We do not show advertisements and we do not use analytics or tracking services.

What other visitors see

When you enter a hall you are given a nickname made of two words, for example “Quiet Dawn”. The people in the same hall can see that nickname, where you stand, and which ritual you are doing. There is no way to type text for others to read. All of this is forgotten when you leave, except that a lamp you lit stays lit for its few minutes, and that for about a minute after a dropped connection your place is held under a random code so that you can rejoin where you were.

What stays in your browser

Your browser's local storage keeps the two words of your nickname, your volume settings and your choices in Settings, so they are there next time. Nothing else is stored, and it is not sent anywhere except the nickname when you join. “Forget my name and settings” in Settings removes it.

What our server records

To keep the temple running we keep technical logs: that a hall was joined or left, how many people are in a hall, counts of refused requests, and error reports from the page (the kind of problem, a short technical note, and the kind of browser such as “chrome-mobile”). They contain random identifiers and hall numbers. They never contain nicknames, names or anything you typed, and we do not record or store your IP address in them.

Our hosting provider

The temple runs on Cloudflare. Like any internet service, Cloudflare handles your IP address when your browser connects, to deliver the page and to protect against abuse. That is governed by Cloudflare's own privacy policy. We use your address only through Cloudflare's rate limiting, which does not give it to us.
